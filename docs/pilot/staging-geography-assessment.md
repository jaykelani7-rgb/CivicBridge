# Cape Town staging geography review — 2026-09-30

**Decision:** no three-source official pilot is ready to ingest. Of the source publications inspected, Cape Town **metro** is the smallest common coverage that does not require assigning one source's aggregate to a smaller area. It is a candidate for a city-level evidence check, not a substitute for ward hotspot scoring: one metro unit cannot rank neighbourhoods against one another. The existing `ZA-WC-CPT-W15` target remains a **synthetic demonstration geography** with an unverified boundary. No official measurements or projects have been loaded into CivicBridge.

## Why the smaller units fail today

- **Ward 15:** the 2024 survey publishes suburb results, not a Ward 15 figure. The available 2022 census page does not publish a Ward 15 profile; its ward profiles are 2011 and explicitly use old demarcations. The budget has ward fields, but no checked Ward 15 water-project extract. The fixture polygon is not an official ward boundary. `docs/pilot/cape-town-source-assessment.json` remains a blocked assessment, not an ingestion manifest.
- **Suburb:** the survey may support one suburb or a published combined-suburb unit, but project budgets are generally ward/point/citywide. The City reports [Hout Bay pipe replacements and a 2026/27 plan](https://web1.capetown.gov.za/web1/newsandnotices/Home/Release/More-than-27-km-of-ageing-water-pipes-replaced-across-the-Deep-South); that announcement does not identify each planned project's ID, Hout Bay allocation, or delivery status. It cannot become a normalized project row or prove that surveyed household tap access represents pipe reliability. Some survey suburbs are combined to meet sample size, so matching a name alone is unsafe.
- **Metro:** city-level survey indicators and citywide budget scope can be compared without splitting aggregates. This is the smallest *documented common coverage*, subject to confirming that the selected budget line really has metro-wide scope. It can establish context or investment alignment only. It cannot explain a Ward 15 score, identify a local hotspot, or apply a suburb-level existing-coverage penalty.

## Exact extracts and mappings needed

1. Download the survey's publisher CSV plus metadata and record the source item/version, URL, retrieval date, raw SHA-256, variable names, units, denominators, weighting method, confidence/uncertainty, suppressed values, and city/suburb identifiers. Select one demographic field and one water-service field with a documented calculation; do not substitute household-sample counts for CivicBridge's `population` denominator. Resolve the dashboard's 2024/2025 fieldwork-date discrepancy with the publisher.
2. Extract the approved budget schedule row(s) with project number, title, water category, approval/status, fiscal-year amounts and units, ward/location/coordinate fields, and exact page or row reference. Distinguish a proposed allocation, an approved allocation, and completed work. For a metro pilot, select only a line whose spatial coverage is actually metro-wide; for a suburb/ward pilot, validate the project's footprint against that unit. Keep a later amendment as a separate version, not a silent overwrite.
3. Acquire the publisher's versioned metro, ward, and (if needed) survey-suburb boundaries. Store original publisher identifiers and a reviewed crosswalk to CivicBridge `geography_id`; validate topology and overlap. A point in a suburb does not show a whole project serves that suburb. Confirm how the survey combines suburbs, and do not apportion a combined result without defensible denominators.
4. Write and review transformations for the existing `demographic_features`, `infrastructure_indices`, and `investment_projects` schemas. In particular, `equity_vulnerability`, `infrastructure_gap`, `strategic_alignment`, `delivery_readiness`, and `existing_coverage_penalty` are 0–100 scoring inputs, not fields established by the cited publications. Leave them unknown until a transparent measure or human assessment is approved; do not invent scores or treat tap access as pipe condition.
5. Document reuse for each exact asset. The City's [Open Data terms](https://www.capetown.gov.za/General/Terms-of-use-open-data) and [Open Data Policy](https://www.capetown.gov.za/_documents/resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Bylaws%20and%20policies/Open_Data_Policy.pdf) describe portal data as available for reuse, but check the survey item's own metadata and attribution terms. The linked budget PDFs and web dashboard are outside that portal's stated scope; confirm their redistribution and derived-data terms with the publisher or counsel before copying them into an open CivicBridge dataset. Do not ingest household-level records or personal information for this pilot.

Staging text/voice and synthetic hotspot tests need not wait for official data.
